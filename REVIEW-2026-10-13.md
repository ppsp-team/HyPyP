# HyPyP 0.6.2: in-depth code review

Date: 2026-10-13. Target: `ppsp-team/HyPyP` at `master` = `dbeeb37` (tag `v0.6.2`, 2026-10-04). Environment: Python 3.13.7, mne 1.11.0, numpy 2.4.3, scipy 1.17.1, autoreject 0.4.3, matplotlib 3.10.8, installed with `uv sync` from the committed lock. No GPU extra (numba, torch, Metal, CuPy) installed, so the accelerated kernels were reviewed by reading only. Nothing in the clone was modified.

## 1. Method

Eight parallel reviews, one per area (stats, analyses, sync, prep/utils/mvarica, wavelet/fNIRS/shiny, xdf/datasets/plots/ext, viz, tests/CI/packaging/docs), with one rule: a claim about a wrong value, wrong formula or crash had to be reproduced on synthetic data before it could be reported, and anything not reproduced is marked as such. Every numeric claim below has a script in `scripts/` next to this file (sub-folders by area). I re-verified in source or by re-running the findings with the widest consequences (marked "re-verified").

Reference points used: Lachaux et al. 1999 (PLV); Jammalamadaka & SenGupta 2001 and Burgess 2013 (circular correlation); Zimmermann et al. 2024, Imaging Neuroscience (adjusted circular correlation); Nolte et al. 2004 (imaginary coherence); Stam et al. 2007 (PLI); Vinck et al. 2011 (wPLI); Torrence & Compo 1998, Torrence & Webster 1999, Grinsted et al. 2004 and Grinsted's `wavelet-coherence` MATLAB toolbox (wavelet coherence); Baccalá & Sameshima 2001 and Kaminski & Blinowska 1991 (PDC/DTF); Palva et al. 2005 (n:m phase locking); MNE 1.11 source for the permutation machinery; pyxdf source for XDF timing.

## 3. Findings that change results silently

Numbered for reference. Severity is mine; "conditional" means the wrong result needs a specific but realistic input.

### Statistics (`hypyp/stats.py`)

**F1. Frequency-channel adjacency is misaligned with the data layout the library produces.** `stats.py:193-205` (`con_matrix`), same pattern at `297-308` and `396-407`. The expansion over frequencies indexes `i // n_ch` as the frequency block, so feature `i = f·n_ch + ch` (frequency-major). `analyses.pow` returns `(n_ch, n_freq)`, which MNE flattens C-order to `ch·n_freq + f` (channel-major). `tests/test_stats.py::test_stats` and the getting-started tutorial feed exactly that 3-D layout to `statscondCluster`. Every adjacency row then describes the wrong feature. Reproduced with 4 channels × 3 frequencies and an effect at channel A, f0 and f2: HyPyP reports one cluster joining (A,f0) and (A,f2), `mne.stats.combine_adjacency(ch_con, n_freq)` reports two. Decoded, the neighbours of (A,f1) under HyPyP's matrix are (A,f0..f2), (B,f0..f2), (C,f0), (C,f1): the row was built for (f0,B). Re-verified in source. Clusters published through this path with more than one frequency have wrong membership and wrong p-values. Fix: build with `mne.stats.combine_adjacency(ch_con, n_freq)` and validate `data.shape[1:]` against the adjacency.

**F2. `statscluster(test="f oneway")` compares only the first two groups.** `stats.py:659-660` calls `scipy.stats.f_oneway(arg[0], arg[1])` while `dfn`/`dfd` and the permutation use all groups. Three groups with only the third different: HyPyP finds one cluster at p = 0.51; scipy on all three gives F of 15 to 27 on the planted features; MNE's default statistic finds two clusters at p = 0.002 and 0.094. Re-verified in source. Fix: `f_oneway(*arg)[0]`.

**F3. The significant-cluster maps drop every cluster that ties on p-value.** `stats.py:520-522` and `707-709` look up `clusters[np.where(cluster_p_values == cluster_p)[0][0]]`, always the first cluster with that p. Ties are the norm for strong effects (both at `1/(n_perm+1)`). Two planted clusters, both p = 0.002: `F_obs_plot` is non-zero at features [1, 2], expected [1, 2, 8, 9]. Re-verified in source. Fix: `for mask, p in zip(clusters, cluster_p_values)`.

**F4. Negative-t clusters vanish from `Stat_obs_plot`.** `stats.py:704-712`: `np.maximum(zeros, mask·Stat_obs)` keeps 0 whenever the cluster's t is negative, so for `ind ttest`/`rel ttest` with `tail=0` every "condition 2 > condition 1" cluster is erased from the plot array while `cluster_p_values` says it is significant. Reproduced: p = 0.002, t of −9 to −13, plot array has zero non-zero entries. Re-verified in source. Fix: `Stat_obs_plot[mask] = Stat_obs[mask]`.

**F5. `statscluster(test="rel ttest")` builds its null without the pairing.** `stats.py:651-652` computes `ttest_rel`, but `permutation_cluster_test` concatenates the two groups and permutes rows, so permuted "pairs" join different subjects and the null variance is var(A) + var(B) instead of var(A − B). Known before this review. The size of the power loss is data-dependent: near-total on EEG-like data where per-subject topography dominates (0 of 20 detections at a lift sign flipping finds 20 of 20, UCLA-R21 2026-10-03), negligible when within-pair noise dominates (10 of 10 in this review's strong-lift regime). Fix: `permutation_cluster_1samp_test(data[0] − data[1])`.

**F6. `statsCond` applies FDR to p-values that MNE already corrected (max-T, FWER), and tests mean ≠ 0 on a strictly positive input.** `stats.py:108-112`. The docstring claims "permutation t-test with FDR"; the result is doubly conservative and the method is misreported. With the documented input (PSD) every channel is trivially significant (t of 32 to 36 on the sample data) because power is never zero. Fix: document that the input must be a contrast, and either compute uncorrected per-channel p before FDR or drop the FDR and document FWER.

**F7. `f multipleway` cannot run with two factors and runs a wrong null with one.** `stats.py:678-685`: `f_threshold_mway_rm(effects="all")` returns one threshold per effect and MNE rejects the list (`TypeError`); a list input hits `AttributeError`; a single factor runs but with between-subject row shuffling under a repeated-measures F, the same defect as F5. Known before this review. Fix: one effect per call with a within-subject permutation scheme, or remove the option.

Other stats findings, all reproduced: `tail=-1` crashes in every path that documents it (`ValueError: incompatible tail and threshold signs`); `alpha` only sets the cluster-forming threshold when `tail == 0` in `statscondCluster` (`498-501`), with `tail=1` MNE's fixed p = 0.05 is used whatever `alpha` is; the three adjacency builders return dense `np.ndarray` while their docstrings say `csr_matrix` and MNE requires sparse (the tests work around it with `bsr_matrix`); `metaconn_matrix_2brains` infers the channel count from `max(electrodes[:,0]) + 1` (`:279`) and raises `IndexError` for any pair list not containing the last channel; the `statscluster` docstring (`604-605`) advises flattening the full `(n_sensors, n_sensors)` matrix, which double-counts every symmetric pair and includes the diagonal; the two result tuples are both named `"statscondCluster"` with fields `F_obs` vs `Stat_obs`; the frequency expansion links spatial neighbours at *neighbouring* frequencies (98 links vs 58 for MNE's convention on 4 × 3), undocumented; the pure-Python O(P²F²) loops take 4 s for 576 pairs × 3 frequencies and would take minutes and gigabytes for a 62-channel inter-brain set (`scipy.sparse.kron` does it in milliseconds).

What is right: the pair-adjacency rule itself (`metaconn_matrix` equals `(a~c & b~d) | (a~d & b~c)`, the two-brain variant equals `(a~c & b~d)` within each brain, cross-brain sensors are never spatial neighbours), the cluster-forming thresholds where they are set, and the unknown-test error added in 0.6.2.

### Analyses (`hypyp/analyses.py`)

**F8. `compute_nmPLV` raises the wrong axis to the n:m power and averages over frequency instead of epochs.** `analyses.py:1034-1052`. The array is laid out `(epoch, freq, 2·n_ch, time)` and the slice is `phase[:, :, :, :n_ch]`, so the first `n_ch` *time samples* of every channel get exponent n and the rest exponent m; participants are never distinguished. The final `nanmean(con, axis=1)` runs without the `swapaxes(0, 1)` that `compute_sync` does, so it averages over frequency and returns `(n_epochs, 2·n_ch, 2·n_ch)` where the docstring promises `(n_freq, 2·n_ch, 2·n_ch)`. Reproduced: participant 1 at 10 Hz, participant 2 at 20 Hz, phase-locked, true 1:2 PLV by hand = 0.999; the function returns inter-brain values of 0.02 to 0.05. Re-verified in source. It also crashes for one epoch or one channel, and emits a `DeprecationWarning` on every call. The existing test checks inequalities between blocks and passes. Separately, the design compares both participants at the *same* frequency bin, whereas Palva et al. 2005 define n:m PLV between φ_x(f1) and φ_y(f2); that is a method decision for a maintainer.

**F9. `compute_conn_mvar`'s "merge epochs" reshape scrambles the data.** `analyses.py:766-771` reshapes `(1, n_epoch, n_freq, 2·n_ch, n_samp)` straight to `(1, n_freq, 2·n_ch, n_samp·n_epoch)`; C-order memory is epoch-major, so the merged channel 0 is epoch-0 channel 0 followed by epoch-0 channel 1. Reproduced on the exact expression. Re-verified in source. Conditional: reached only through the interactive "yes" branch, but a user who takes it fits a VAR on garbage and gets a plausible PDC.

**F10. `xwt` wavelet coherence does not implement the smoothing it claims and its null is inflated.** `analyses.py:1166-1167` builds a Gaussian of std = scale truncated to length `int(2·s)`, i.e. ±1σ, and applies no smoothing across scales (Grinsted eq. 9: boxcar of 0.6·scale). Independent white noise: HyPyP mean WTC 0.660, 95th percentile 0.968; a reference with the same ±1σ truncation reproduces it (0.654 / 0.966); full Gaussian 0.465 / 0.868; full Gaussian plus scale smoothing 0.332 / 0.735. Published WTC significance thresholds (0.7 to 0.8) are meaningless at this bias. Re-verified in source. Note this is the EEG `xwt` in `analyses.py`, distinct from the fNIRS wavelet package, which smooths correctly (see F13 for its own issue).

Other analyses findings, all reproduced: `compute_single_freq` excludes `fmax` (`[8, 12]` gives 4 frequencies, docstring says "8 to 30 gives 23"); the coherence docstring claims magnitude-squared while the code returns magnitude (0.9034 vs 0.8162 on the same pair); `indices_connectivity_intrabrain`/`_interbrain` return wrong pairs when EOG channels are present because they index into the EOG-stripped list (`('C1_S1', 'EOG_S1')` and `('C2_S1', 'C1_S2')` appear); `pow` mutates the caller's Epochs (`drop_channels` on the argument, `101-103`); `compute_conn_mvar` calls `input()` and raises `EOFError` under non-interactive stdin, returns `None` or a string on a declared `np.ndarray` signature, and documents `n_channels` where the axis is `2·n_channels`; `xwt` crashes on one epoch, returns a *string* for an unknown mode and recomputes every CWT `2·n_ch²` times; `behav_corr` returns `None` for a shape it does not handle; `plt.ion()` at import (`:28`) switches matplotlib to interactive mode for every importer; the PLI/wPLI NumPy paths build an `(E, F, 2n, 2n, T)` complex intermediate (a 4 MB input peaks at 660 MB; 64 channels × 100 epochs × 1000 samples would need ~26 GB).

Two documentation gaps that matter for comparability: `compute_sync` computes every metric *within each epoch* and averages epoch values, so the bias of independent signals depends on epoch length, not recording length (80 s of independent 8 to 12 Hz noise cut into 1 s / 4 s / 16 s epochs gives PLV 0.324 / 0.150 / 0.077). Neither `pair_connectivity` nor `compute_sync` says so. And `compute_single_freq` uses `n_cycles=4` with MNE's default `time_bandwidth=4`, so "single frequency" bins overlap strongly (an 8 or 12 Hz tone keeps 32 % of its power at the 10 Hz bin).

What is right: on synthetic coupling, identical signals give plv/ccorr/accorr/coh/envcorr/powcorr = 1.000 and imcoh/pli/wpli = 0.000; an exact 90° shift gives imcoh/pli/wpli = 1.000; the inter-brain block is sliced from the correct quadrant with `[i, j]` = (p1 channel i, p2 channel j); PDC/DTF direction is `[i, j]` = from j to i, matching Baccalá & Sameshima 2001; inputs are not mutated by `compute_freq_bands` or `compute_sync`.

### Preprocessing and utilities (`prep.py`, `utils.py`, `mvarica.py`)

**F11. `AR_local(strategy="intersection")` silently misaligns the dyad.** `prep.py:457-480`. Only the intersection of the two bad-epoch sets is dropped from both participants; then `ar.transform(epochs_subj)` recomputes each participant's own reject log and drops that participant's remaining bad epochs; `equalize_epoch_counts` afterwards only equalises counts. Reproduced with 40 epochs, bad epoch 5 planted in S1 only and bad epoch 9 in S2 only: lengths [39, 39], S1 keeps 0..4, 6..; S2 keeps 0..8, 10..; epoch index 5 of S1 is paired with a different time window of S2. Every inter-brain measure computed afterwards is on non-simultaneous data, and nothing checks `events[:, 0]`. The docstring says those epochs are "repaired individually"; they are dropped. Re-verified in source. Fix: pass a reject log with `bad_epochs` all False to `ar.transform(..., reject_log=...)` so only interpolation happens, and assert identical `events[:, 0]` across participants at the end. The `"union"` strategy is alignment-safe (verified), as is `ICA_fit`/`ICA_apply`.

**F12. `generate_virtual_epoch` does not integrate the coupling it promises.** `utils.py:668`: `solve_ivp` with default `rtol=1e-3` on an unbounded phase (≈550 rad after 8 s) allows ~0.5 rad per step; RK45 takes 230 evaluations for 2000 output samples. With `W[2,0]=20` and |ω0 − ω2| = 2.47 rad/s (must lock): PLV(0,2) = 0.354 at defaults, 1.000 with `rtol=1e-9` or `max_step=1/sfreq`. Through the library call, the coupled pair gives PLV 0.235 while an uncoupled pair gives 0.662. Re-verified by re-running. Any "ground truth" validation done with this simulator is invalid. Fix: `max_step=1/sfreq`; move the in-RHS noise term to a fixed-step Euler-Maruyama loop or drop it; also `np.linspace(0, tmax, Nt)` gives dt off by Nt/(Nt−1), and the first n/2 channels all start at phase 0.

Other findings, all reproduced: `epochs_from_tasks` is wrong when `raw.first_samp != 0` (`TASK_BEGINNING` returns 0 epochs silently; `TASK_END` gives tmax 4.99 instead of 8.0); `merge` with different sampling rates or epoch lengths returns NaN-filled data (3600 of 14472 NaN for 100 vs 200 Hz) that downstream `nanmean` then hides; MVARICA output indices are ICA sources, not channels, and the mixing matrix is computed and discarded, so links come back as an unrecoverable permutation of the true ones while the docstring says "from channel j to channel i"; `MVAR` with `delta != 0` crashes (`construct_equation` called with the wrong arity); `MVAR.stability()` crashes for `model_order=1`; `AR_local` reads `reject_logs[0]` and `[1]` only, so a third participant's bad epochs are ignored and alignment is lost; `create_epochs` uses inclusive `tmax=duration` so epochs share a sample and the last one is dropped; `filt`, `merge` (via `interpolate_bads`) and `ICA_apply` mutate their inputs undocumented; `merge` drops `tmin`, events and montage; `ICA_choice_comp` blocks on `input()`; MVAR residuals keep `p` raw samples per epoch and feed them to the ICA; `generate_random_epoch` and `generate_virtual_epoch` use the unseeded global `np.random`.

### Wavelet and fNIRS (`hypyp/wavelet`, `hypyp/fnirs`)

**F13. CWT cache key collision on identical subject labels scrambles the inter-subject matrix on the default path.** `base_wavelet.py:466-495` keys the CWT cache on the subject label, channel, task, epoch and time range; `recording.py:173-180` takes the label from `subject_info["his_id"]`; both bundled DCARE SNIRF files carry `his_id == "default"` (re-verified), caching is on by default, and `Dyad.compute_wtcs(wavelet=None)` creates a caching `ComplexMorletWavelet()`. After the first row of the channel cross product, every lookup of `[default][ch]` returns subject 2's CWT, so rows ≥ 1 are subject-2 *intra* coherences reported as inter-subject. Reproduced on three channels: without cache the matrix is 0.22 to 0.25 throughout; with the default wavelet row 2 reads `0.254 1.000 0.500` and row 3 `0.234 0.500 1.000`. `tests/test_fnirs.py:403-411` exercises exactly this collision and checks only counts; the fNIRS tutorial never sets `subject_label` and shares one `cache=dict()` across the whole study. Fix: give each `Recording` a unique id at load, carry it in `PairSignals` and the cache key, and raise in `Dyad.__init__` when two distinct recordings share a label.

**F14 (conditional). pywt's CWT kernel degrades to a staircase at large scales; the WTC is then wrong for broadband or high-rate signals.** `pywavelets_wavelet.py:89-96` calls `pywt.cwt(method="fft")` with the default `precision=12` (4096-point wavelet, step 0.0039). pywt builds the scaled kernel by integer-indexing the integrated wavelet (`pywt/_cwt.py:149-153`, re-verified), so once `scale·step` approaches 1 (scale ≳ 250 samples) the kernel alternates weights and above 256 samples contains zeros. Envelope error vs a smooth Morlet: scale 20 → 0.08, 100 → 0.33, 200 → 0.50, ≥ 500 → 1.00. Against an independent CWT with identical smoothing: 10 Hz white noise mean |Δ| 0.010; 50 Hz white noise 0.133 (max 0.749); 50 Hz band-limited 0.02 to 0.7 Hz 0.0001; bundled DCARE at 7.8 Hz 0.0006; `cwt_params=dict(precision=16)` at 50 Hz 0.0017. Pure sinusoids pass to 0.1 % at every scale, which is why nothing in the suite sees it. The documented fNIRS path (band-passed HbO at ~8 Hz, periods ≤ 20 s) is safe; unfiltered `MnePreprocessorAsIs` data at ≥ 25 Hz, EEG envelopes, or a period range extended to 100 s at 10 Hz are silently wrong. Fix: compute the Morlet CWT in the Fourier domain (T&C eq. 6, as pycwt does), or set `precision` from `max(scales)` and raise when `2**precision` is not ≫ 16·max_scale.

**F15. Every fNIRS pair's `dt` is wrong by n/(n−1).** `dyad.py:131`: `x = np.linspace(0, n/sfreq, n)` (also `shiny/wavelet_explorer.py:171,261`, `matlab_wavelet.py:88`). `PairSignals.dt = x[1] − x[0]` feeds scales, periods, times, COI and `WTC.sfreq`. A 20 s epoch at 7.8125 Hz gives `WTC.sfreq = 7.763`; all reported periods and the COI are 0.6 % short on a 20 s epoch, 1.3 % on 10 s. Re-verified in source. Fix: `np.arange(n)/sfreq`.

Other wavelet/fNIRS findings, all reproduced: the scale-smoothing boxcar is twice Grinsted's width (`base_wavelet.py:414-417`, a `*2` carried over from pycwt with a TODO; white-noise mean R² 0.223 vs 0.321 at Grinsted's width; docstring says 0.6 "in scale"), so literature significance thresholds do not transfer; the default pipeline (`MnePreprocessorAsIs`, `ch_match=None`) correlates raw light intensities and pairs 760 nm with 850 nm, and with haemoglobin data pairs HbO with HbR and averages across chromophores in every pivot; intra-subject rows are duplicated in `Study.df` when a Recording belongs to more than one dyad; pseudo-dyads enumerate all n(n−1) `(s1_i, s2_j)` combinations with no identity check, so when a participant recurs the real dyads reappear flagged `is_pseudo=True` and contaminate the surrogate distribution; the COI for `ComplexGaussianWavelet` uses the DOG Fourier factor while the period axis uses pywt's, over-masking by ~1.6×; the period axis is off by the T&C Fourier factor (1.25 %) and ω0 = 2π rather than 6, undocumented; `keep_wtcs=False` clears `s1.intra_wtcs` twice and never `s2`; trailing partial time bins are dropped silently; the global `coherence_metric` ignores the mask threshold the bins use (a 20 s epoch at the default 2 to 20 s period range is 76 % masked, bins come back NaN, the global metric 0.257); `block_reduce` pads the last block with the global mean; a constant channel gives NaN with no message; string `ch_match` is a substring match (`"S1_D1"` also matches `S1_D10`); three optional modules are wrapped in bare `except:` so a broken wrapper is skipped, never detected; random subject labels are unseeded and land in result tables; `fnirs/legacy/fnirs_tools.py` is dead code that writes and deletes files in the current directory.

What is right: the coherence normalisation, the Gaussian time smoothing and the division by scale before smoothing match Grinsted 2004 / Torrence & Webster 1999; identical inputs give R² = 1 to 1e-15 everywhere; end-to-end against an independent T&C-1998 CWT with identical smoothing, mean |Δ| = 0.004 on 10 Hz data; `ChannelROI` ordering is correct; pseudo-dyads use real recordings, preserving autocorrelation; NaN runs split cleanly.

### XDF import (`hypyp/xdf`)

**F16. `Markers.__add__` concatenates `self.timestamps` with itself.** `xdf_stream.py:57`. Any file with two or more marker streams (the shipped `dyad-example-noise.xdf` has one per headset) yields merged markers whose second-stream timestamps are the first stream's values while the descriptions are right. `Markers([1,2]) + Markers([100,200])` gives timestamps `[1, 2, 1, 2]`. `tests/test_xdf.py:236-240` checks the length only. Re-verified in source. Fix: `np.concatenate([self.timestamps, other.timestamps])`, then sort.

**F17. The Raw time axis ignores gaps and the effective rate; markers mapped through timestamps then diverge from the data.** `xdf_stream.py:204,303,316` build `RawArray(data, sfreq=nominal_srate)` from the bare sample array; pyxdf's `info["segments"]` and `info["effective_srate"]` are never read. 100 Hz with a 6 s gap and a marker at +12 s: the Raw is 9.99 s long and the sample recorded at +12 s sits at t = 6.00 s, so the marker is off by the gap and `set_annotations` keeps 0 of them. Below pyxdf's break threshold a 2 s gap is fitted by one line: effective rate 73.99 Hz, marker onset 8.65 s on a 7.99 s Raw. On the shipped `data_with_clock_resets.xdf`: nominal 100 Hz, effective 92.93 Hz, two segments, 83 of 174 markers fall after the Raw ends. For a dyad, a gap in one amplifier shifts every later sample of that participant relative to the other by the gap length, silently. Fix: build each Raw on a regular grid from the dejittered timestamps (NaN or zeros plus a `BAD_gap` annotation in the holes), or at minimum raise when `len(segments) > 1` or the effective rate deviates from nominal.

**F18. No sample-level alignment between the two Raws, while the tutorial hands them over as ready.** Each Raw has `times[0] = 0` at its own first sample (`xdf_stream.py:208,303`); nothing crops to the common interval. Sample 0 of EEG1 is true time 0.0 and sample 0 of EEG2 is 0.5 s in the synthetic case; on the shipped files the first-sample offset is 1.8 ms (noise file) and 13.8 ms with 6536 vs 6538 samples (markers file, 1000 Hz: 14 samples, about 50° of alpha phase). `tutorial/import_from_xdf.ipynb` cell 8 ("convert all") presents `mne_raws` as ready; no docstring or test mentions `reference_time` offsets. Fix: an `aligned_raws()` helper that crops both to `[max(t0), min(t_end)]` on the regular grid of F17.

**F19. `scale="auto"` multiplies by `10e-6`, which is 1e-5.** `xdf_stream.py:372`. Neither µV→V (1e-6) nor mV→V (1e-3). 20 µV noise comes out at 2.0e-4 V (10× too large). Coherence and PLV are scale-invariant; amplitude thresholds, autoreject, ICA and plots are not. Re-verified in source. Fix: `1e-6`, and read the XDF `<unit>` tag (the shipped markers file declares `µV`) before guessing.

Other XDF findings, all reproduced: annotations are never attached to the Raws because `xdf_import.py:248-253` tests `data_stream.markers`, which is set only on marker streams (the docstring, the tutorial and `datasets.py:371` claim otherwise; on the shipped markers file, 12 markers, 0 annotations on either Raw); `as_mne_annotations` assumes sorted timestamps and drops everything before the first non-negative onset; `select_matches` integers are XDF stream ids but documented as indices (`select_matches=[3]` on the noise file silently selects the markers stream); a declared signal stream with no samples crashes the default import; `datasets.fetch(force_download=True)` is a no-op; the `time_series` orientation heuristic transposes wrongly when `n_samples == n_channels` and crashes when fewer; duplicate stream names collapse `mne_raws_dict` and name matching; `verbose or True` always prints; the `"auto"` scale pools accelerometer and counter channels into its statistics. In `hypyp/ext/mpl3d`, `mesh.py:65,67` reference undefined names in `mode="back"` (`NameError`), `lighting.py` is dead, imports were rewritten (so "kept as upstream wrote it" is not literal), and there is no `LICENSE` file for Rougier's BSD code or the SGI trackball notice.

What is right: clock-domain synchronisation is correctly delegated to pyxdf (`synchronize_clocks`, `dejitter_timestamps`, `handle_clock_resets` all on); a stream with a +10 s clock offset came back with `reference_time` exactly 0.5 s after the other; channel-name lookup handles both the spec and the common vendor layout; the dataset registry has a sha256 for every file and a configurable cache.

### Visualisation (`hypyp/viz.py`)

**F20 (conditional). ### Sync metrics (`hypyp/sync`)

No finding that changes a value on the default NumPy path. Items worth acting on:

- README and docstrings state magnitude-squared coherence and signed ImCoh and CCorr; the code returns |C| (0.8361 where MSC is 0.6991), |Im C| and |ρ_c|. A reader following the docs reports a different quantity than they computed, and a modulus has a positive bias under the null that a signed value does not. Fix the formulas in `sync/README.md:31-33,67-70,84-86`, `coh.py:38`, `imaginary_coh.py:39`, `ccorr.py`, `analyses.py:538-541`.
- Backends disagree on degenerate channels: NumPy gives NaN on a zero-power channel, numba and CUDA kernels return 0.0, torch is mixed. Since `compute_sync` then `nanmean`s over epochs, a zeroed bad channel is *excluded* on NumPy but *averaged in as zero connectivity* on the accelerated paths, with no warning on either. Choose one convention and apply it in every kernel. (Kernel side read from source; not run here.)
- `priority` lists do not do what the docs say: `'numba'` and `'numpy'` are never selected inside the loop, `priority=['numpy']` on a GPU machine gives numba with the false warning "No GPU backend available", and on CPU-only machines `priority` is never read. `'auto'` picks float32 Metal or MPS on Apple Silicon and float64 elsewhere, with 1e-2 tolerances in the tests for PLI/wPLI: a published value can depend on the laptop. Metal without torch is never chosen by `'auto'`.
- PLV and ACCorr normalise by |z|, so one zero sample wipes a whole channel row to NaN and `compute_sync` silently averages the remaining epochs.
- EnvCorr is plain Pearson on envelopes, not orthogonalised; citing Hipp et al. 2012 implies otherwise. wPLI is the non-debiased estimator (0.674 vs 0.454 debiased on the same data). All nine metrics take the time statistic within an epoch and apply |·| per epoch; the README marks that only for PLV. State all three once.
- `tests/test_sync.py:584` defines `class TestEnvCorr` and `:900` redefines it, so the first five tests are never collected; two wPLI tests live inside `TestPowCorr`; `test_wpli_numba_vs_numpy` is also gated on torch.

What is right: all nine formulas reproduce independent scalar references (np.angle, np.corrcoef, scipy circmean, hand loops) to ten digits; constant lag π/4 gives PLV = PLI = wPLI = Coh = CCorr = ACCorr = 1 and ImCoh = 0.7071; the ACCorr denominator matches Zimmermann et al. 2024 eq. 1.2 exactly and the identity Σ sin(α−m) sin(β−n) = (R− − R+)/2 holds to 1e-14; numba, CUDA and torch kernels implement the same arithmetic as NumPy with float64 accumulators (by reading); dispatch fails closed on an unknown backend; the tests spy on kernel entry so a fallback cannot make a backend test vacuous.

## 5. Method decisions for a maintainer (not bugs)

These are places where the code makes a defensible choice that differs from the cited reference or from what the docs say, and where the fix is a decision plus a sentence of documentation, not a patch:

1. Coherence returns |C|, not |C|²; ImCoh and CCorr return the modulus, not the signed value. Keep or change, but say which, and note the positive null bias of a modulus.
2. `compute_sync` averages within-epoch statistics across epochs (the single-trial form of Lachaux 2000 / Mormann 2000), not across trials at each time point (Lachaux 1999). Bias depends on epoch length. State it in `pair_connectivity` and `compute_sync`.
3. wPLI is not debiased (Vinck 2011 eq. 8, not the debiased estimator). EnvCorr is not orthogonalised (Hipp 2012 is cited). Say so.
4. The wavelet scale-smoothing window is twice Grinsted's; the Morlet ω0 is 2π, not 6; the period axis uses pywt's convention (1.25 % off T&C's). Each shifts significance thresholds taken from the literature.
5. n:m PLV compares both participants at the same frequency bin; Palva 2005 compares φ_x(f1) with φ_y(f2).
6. `compute_single_freq` bins overlap strongly at `n_cycles=4`, `time_bandwidth=4`.
7. The frequency-expanded adjacency links spatial neighbours at neighbouring frequencies (diagonal links), unlike MNE and FieldTrip.
8. Pseudo-dyads are a deterministic complete enumeration, not a sampled surrogate; no seed, no `n_pseudo`, no identity check.
9. `statsCond` runs a one-sample sign-flip test, so its input must be a contrast; the docstring and tutorial pass raw power.
